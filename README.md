The Nomvula Bird — By A.C. Jordan
A terrible drought dried up all the rivers in the valley. The animals were dying of thirst, so they gathered to dig a deep well. Everyone worked hard under the hot sun, except for Hare. Hare laughed at them and refused to touch a spade.
When the well filled with cold, sweet water, the King of Animals declared, “Hare did not dig, so Hare shall not drink.”
The King left the Nomvula Bird to guard the well. Later that day, Hare walked over carrying a small clay pot. The Nomvula Bird hopped in front of the well and chirped fiercely, “Stay back, lazy Hare! No work, no water!”
Hare smiled and pulled out a piece of sweet honeycomb. “I do not want your muddy well water,” Hare lied. “I have this magic liquid. It is sweeter than anything on earth.”
The bird was curious. “Let me taste just a drop,” it begged.
Hare gave the bird a tiny taste. The Nomvula Bird closed its eyes in delight. “More, please!” it cried.
“I will give you the whole honeycomb,” Hare said, “but only if you let me tie your wings behind your back. If you eat this magic food, you will fly into the sun, and I must keep you safe.”
The foolish bird agreed. Hare tied the bird’s wings tightly with a rope, drank his fill from the well, filled his pot, and ran away laughing. When the animals returned, they found their guard tied up and the water stolen.
